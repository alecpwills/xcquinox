\paragraph{$F_c$/$F_x$ asymmetry.} While integration tightens $F_x$ on every
architecture (8/8 step-5 + 2/2 step-6 = 10/10), $F_c$ is mixed: 5 of 8
architectures (\texttt{deep\_dm}, \texttt{deep\_dm\_attn},
\texttt{deep\_cusp\_attn}, \texttt{deep\_combined},
\texttt{deep\_combined\_attn}) are \emph{worse} under integration on $F_c$.
The 3 that benefit (\texttt{deep}, \texttt{deep\_attn}, \texttt{deep\_cusp})
include the two architectures with the largest absolute unweighted $F_c$
baselines ($1.15\!\times\!10^{-4}$ and $9.99\!\times\!10^{-5}$); their
ratios (1.63 and 1.30 in favour of integration) dominate the population mean.
Likely causes: (a) PW92 $\varepsilon_c$ is roughly an order of magnitude
smaller in magnitude than $\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{LDA}}$ at typical bonding
densities, narrowing the $F_c$-weighted residual's dynamic range, and (b)
PBE's $F_c$ functional form is closer to the LDA limit than $F_x$ is in the
GGA regime, leaving less ``room to move'' under any re-weighting.

\section{Optimization step 5: H$_2$O training}
\label{S:sec:step5}

Step 5 trains the $F_x$ network on H$_2$O alone for 250 task-loss steps
across the full 8-architecture~$\times$~3-loss~$\times$~3-solver factorial
(72 specs per pretrain origin; 144 specs total). The headline numbers
(Tab.~\ref{S:tab:step5_headline}) pair unweighted and integration origins
on every metric the manuscript discusses; the post-processing figures
(figs5\_1 through figs5\_5) drill into per-architecture pretrain quality,
the AE-baseline reduction ladder, the density-vs-AE Pareto cloud, the
full architecture~$\times$~loss~$\times$~solver landscape, and the
transfer-mol breakdown on $\{$CH$_4$, H$_2$, OH$\}$. The in-notebook
diagnostics (\S\ref{S:sec:step5} subsection \emph{In-notebook diagnostics})
add training-loss curves, GradNorm weight-evolution traces, SCF
convergence aggregates, density-matrix and grid-density heatmaps,
$F_x(s)$ drift on the CH$_4$ probe grid, per-mol transfer detail, and a
feature-impact analysis.

\section{Optimization step 6: H$_2$O+C$_2$H$_2$ training, paired origins}
\label{S:sec:step6}

Step 6 expands step 5 along three axes simultaneously: (i) two molecules
in the training set (H$_2$O + C$_2$H$_2$, adding sp~hybridization and
$\pi$-bonding), (ii) five $V_{xc}$-aware loss families instead of the
three exchange-and-density-only families of step 5, and (iii) three
training-step schedules (group~1: H$_2$O short; group~2: H$_2$O+C$_2$H$_2$
short; group~3: H$_2$O+C$_2$H$_2$ long), giving 90 specs per pretrain
origin. This section presents the unweighted and integration origins
side-by-side: the headline table at \S\ref{S:sec:step6_headline}, the six
post-processing figures (\texttt{fig1}--\texttt{fig5} plus the secondary
transfer-set ladder \texttt{fig1b}) paired by origin in
\S\ref{S:sec:step6_post_processing}, and the in-notebook diagnostics in
\S\ref{S:sec:step6_in_notebook}.

\subsection{Step-6 in-notebook diagnostics}
\label{S:sec:step6_in_notebook}

The step-6 notebook (\texttt{notebooks/\_build\_step6\_notebook.py})
produces these diagnostics during the run, independent of the
post-processing analysis. They cover pretrain dynamics, anchor and
balancing-loss sensitivity, $F_x(s)$ profiles on probe-molecule grids
(panel B = CH$_4$ + C$_2$H$_2$; panel C = held-out C$_2$H$_4$),
per-group training-loss curves, SCF convergence, transfer-set MAE

\section{Pretrain-origin comparison (step 6)}
\label{S:sec:origin_comparison}

This section pairs the two step-6 pretrain origins
(\S\ref{S:sec:origin_comparison_direct}) and presents four cross-cutting
figures that quantify where integration weighting wins, where it loses,
and how the learned $F_x$ asymptote behaves at large reduced gradient.
\S\ref{S:sec:new_diagnostics} adds two newly-derived diagnostics
(F$_x$/F$_c$ tightening correlation across step-5 architectures, and
the per-loss g3 MAE ratio bar chart) that clarify two load-bearing
claims of the main paper.

\subsection{Direct comparison}
\label{S:sec:origin_comparison_direct}

The two step-6 origins differ only in the pretrain-loss weighting:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{unweighted}: $w_x \!=\! w_c \!=\! 1$ in
  \texttt{xcquinox/alec/pretrain.py}.
\item \textbf{integration}: $w_x \!=\! |\rho \varepsilon_x^{\text{LDA}}|$
  with the Dirac--Slater LDA exchange energy density
  \citep{Dirac1930, Slater1951}; $w_c \!=\! |\rho \varepsilon_c^{\text{PW92}}|$
  \citep{PerdewWang1992}.
\end{itemize}
The paired headline numbers are in Tab.~\ref{S:tab:step6_paired}.

\section{Lieb--Oxford / PBE-design ceiling enforcement audit}
\label{S:sec:lo_audit}

The architectural \texttt{\_AlecLOB} clamp at
\texttt{xcquinox/alec/networks.py:42--44} guarantees
$F_x \in [0, 1.804]$ at every grid point regardless of training history.
We verify this empirically by post-processing every step-6 checkpoint
with \texttt{notebooks/analysis/audit\_lob\_enforcement.py}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Lieb--Oxford / PBE-design ceiling enforcement audit. Across the 90
step-6 specs in each pretrain origin (180 total checkpoints), the global
maximum $F_x$ is exactly 1.804000 to six decimals --- the design ceiling,
never exceeded. Zero specs in either origin produce any $F_x > 1.804$ at
any grid point. The minimum-$F_x$ value hits the lower sigmoid floor in
low-density tail regions.}
\label{S:tab:lo_audit}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
quantity & unweighted & integration \\
\midrule
\# checkpoints scanned & 90 & 90 \\
global $\max_g F_x$ & 1.804000 & 1.804000 \\
global $\min_g F_x$ & 0.000 & 0.0335 \\
specs with $F_x > 1.804$ & 0 / 90 & 0 / 90 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The \texttt{\_AlecLOB.limit} field is declared
\texttt{eqx.field(static=True)} at \texttt{xcquinox/alec/networks.py:21},
so gradient descent has no path to mutate the ceiling --- the bound is
enforced at the level of the computation graph, not by post-hoc projection.
Recall (main \S1) that 1.804 is PBE's stricter design choice; the bare
Lieb--Oxford bound permits $F_x \le 2.273$
\citep{LiebOxford1981, PerdewBurkeErnzerhof1996}.
