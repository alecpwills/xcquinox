\begin{frame}{The objects named in this talk}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cell:} one trained exchange-correlation functional (an exchange and a correlation network), identified by its architecture and the number $r$ of training points it was fitted to. \textbf{Subset $r$:} the $r$ points of the 26-point DFS pool [DFS21] chosen for that column of cells (Sec. 2).

    \item \textbf{Pre-training:} fitting a network to reproduce its parent functional (PBE or SCAN) before any reference data is seen. \textbf{Certificate:} the check that the clone reproduces the parent to tolerance; a failing certificate holds the group's training.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoints:} training saves the final, the lowest-training-loss and the lowest-validation-error network; every figure reads the \textbf{validation-best} one.

\begin{frame}{The measures named in this talk}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{WTMAD-2:} GMTKN55's weighted total mean absolute deviation of reaction energies [Goe17]: each benchmark's mean absolute error divided by its mean absolute reference value and scaled by 56.84 kcal/mol; two benchmarks are present here (BH76 barriers, W4-11 atomization energies). Lower is better; the pooled PBE value is 8.92 kcal/mol.
    \item \textbf{$\varepsilon_n$:} the DFS density error [DFS21 Eq. 20], the integrated absolute difference from the CCSD reference density per electron (PBE 0.00923). \textbf{Grid RMSE:} the root-mean-square density difference over the quadrature grid, the measure the density loss trains on.
    \item \textbf{$\mathcal{ED}$:} the harmonic mean of the energy error and $\gamma$ times the density error [DFS21 Eq. 21]; \textbf{DFS units} use $\varepsilon_n$ with $\gamma = 1084.87$ kcal/mol, \textbf{self-calibrated units} the grid RMSE with $\gamma$ set so that PBE's two legs are equal.
    \item \textbf{Beats PBE / cap:} a cell is below PBE when its $\mathcal{ED}$ is below PBE's on the same held-out slice; on the bar charts PBE's value on the cell's own slice is the black cap and a triangle marks a cell below it.
    \item \textbf{Recurring tail:} the species whose density error exceeds 1.5 times PBE's in at least half of the cells carrying them and in at least two (eight species). \textbf{T1:} the CCSD T1 diagnostic of multireference character; 38 of the 189 held-out species are above 0.02, the model-free counterpart of the tail.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{1. Model}

\begin{frame}{1. Model: density variables and the DFS coordinates}
  With $n = n_\alpha + n_\beta$ and $\sigma = |\nabla n|^2$ [DFS21 Eqs. 4, 5]:
  \[
  k_F = (3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}, \qquad
  s = \frac{|\nabla n|}{2 k_F n}, \qquad
  r_s = \left(\frac{3}{4\pi n}\right)^{1/3},
  \]
  \[
  \zeta = \frac{n_\alpha - n_\beta}{n}, \qquad
  \phi(\zeta) = \tfrac12\left[(1+\zeta)^{4/3} + (1-\zeta)^{4/3}\right].
  \]
  The networks read logarithmic compressions of these [DFS21 Eqs. 7 to 10; code: networks.py:621--657]:
  \[
  x_0 = \ln\!\left(n^{1/3} + 10^{-5}\right), \qquad
  x_1 = \ln \phi(\zeta), \qquad
  x_s = \left(1 - e^{-s^2}\right)\ln(s + 1), \qquad
  x_\alpha = \ln\frac{\alpha + 1}{2}.
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Exchange input $[x_s, \ldots]$ with $s$ of the doubled spin channel $2 n_\sigma$ (the exchange network is evaluated per spin channel, next slides); correlation input $[x_0, x_1, x_s, \ldots]$ on the total density. The ellipsis holds the architecture's extra descriptors; on the meta-GGA rung the raw $\alpha$ is replaced by $x_\alpha$.
    \item The $10^{-5}$ in $x_0$ is the DFS reference implementation's constant [dpyscf net.py:39; code: networks.py:27].
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the first two display lines define the density variables: $k_F$ the Fermi wave vector of a uniform gas at density $n$, $s$ the reduced (dimensionless) density gradient, $r_s$ the Wigner-Seitz radius (large where the density is low), $\zeta$ the spin polarization (0 closed shell, $\pm 1$ fully polarized) and $\phi$ the spin-scaling factor of PBE correlation. The third line gives the coordinates the networks receive: logarithmic compressions of $n^{1/3}$ (which spans many orders of magnitude on a molecular grid), of $\phi$, of $s$ (the form $x_s$ behaves as $s^2 \ln(s+1)$ near $s = 0$ and as $\ln s$ at large $s$) and of $\alpha$ on the meta-GGA rung. The bullets state which coordinates each network reads and the origin of the $10^{-5}$ constant. \par \textbf{The point:} the networks see order-one inputs everywhere on the grid, and exchange sees each spin channel separately at twice its density, which is how the exact spin-scaling relation of exchange is built in (frame 8). \par \textbf{Terms:} $k_F$, $s$, $r_s$, $\zeta$, $\phi$, the DFS coordinates $x_0$, $x_1$, $x_s$, $x_\alpha$. \par \textbf{If asked:} where the constant comes from: the DFS reference implementation's net.py line 39, reproduced at networks.py:27. What happens at vanishing density: the density is floored at a small cutoff before any variable is formed, so the grid's vacuum tail produces no infinity. Why the correlation network reads the total density and the polarization: PW92 correlation, whose enhancement factor the network multiplies, is a function of $r_s$ and $\zeta$ (frame 8). \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p5_1_k_f_s_and_the_density_floor_exchange_net} to \pageref{code:p5_7_the_exchange_network_on_the_doubled_spin}.}

\begin{frame}{1. Model: the extra descriptors}
  \textbf{Iso-orbital indicator} (meta-GGA rung; SCAN's $\alpha$ [SCAN15 Eq. 2; DFS21 Eq. 6; code: metagga.py:8--9]), a contraction of the live density matrix $P$ against the AO gradients, hence self-consistent and differentiable through the SCF:
  \[
  \alpha = \frac{\tau - \tau_W}{\tau_{\rm unif}}, \qquad
  \tau = \tfrac12 \sum_i |\nabla\psi_i|^2 = \tfrac12 \sum_{\mu\nu} P_{\mu\nu}\, \nabla\chi_\mu\cdot\nabla\chi_\nu,
  \]
  \[
  \tau_W = \frac{|\nabla n|^2}{8n}, \qquad
  \tau_{\rm unif} = \tfrac{3}{10}(3\pi^2)^{2/3} n^{5/3},
  \]
  with $\alpha \ge 0$ imposed by a smooth positive part rather than a hard clip [code: metagga.py:25--31].
  \vspace{0.1em}

  \textbf{Nuclear cusp pair} (\texttt{cusp}), two bounded geometric features per grid point [code: descriptors.py:230--245]:
  \[
  x_4 = \exp(-2 Z_{\rm nearest}\, r_{\min}), \qquad
  x_5 = \tanh\!\left(\frac{1}{5}\ln \sum_A \frac{Z_A}{r_A}\right),
  \]
  $x_4$ the density-form cusp envelope $e^{-2Zr}$ [Steiner63, after Kato57], $x_5$ the log-compressed bare-nuclear potential. Both are geometric, so the synthetic pre-training mesh (Sec. 3) carries no values for them.
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the upper block defines the iso-orbital indicator $\alpha$ of the meta-GGA rung: $\tau$ is the positive kinetic-energy density of the occupied orbitals, on a basis set a contraction of the density matrix $P$ with the gradients of the atomic-orbital functions, which is why it is differentiable through the self-consistent field; $\tau_W$ is the von Weizsacker value (a single occupied orbital) and $\tau_{\rm unif}$ the uniform-gas value at the same density, so $\alpha$ reads 0 in a single-orbital region, 1 in a uniform-gas-like region and grows where closed shells overlap; the smooth positive part keeps the derivative continuous where $\alpha \ge 0$ is violated numerically, which a hard clip would not. The lower block defines the cusp pair: $x_4$ is 1 at a nucleus and decays with the density's own cusp envelope $e^{-2Zr}$, $x_5$ a bounded, log-compressed bare nuclear potential summed over the nuclei. \par \textbf{The point:} the two descriptor families that extend the GGA inputs are both physical: one reads the kinetic-energy density through the live density matrix, the other the geometry of the nuclei. \par \textbf{Terms:} iso-orbital indicator, kinetic-energy density, von Weizsacker term, the nuclear cusp, the cusp pair. \par \textbf{If asked:} why the synthetic pre-training mesh carries no cusp values: the mesh is a grid of $(r_s, s, \alpha)$ values tied to no molecule, and $x_4$, $x_5$ depend only on the geometry, so those rows have nothing to give them (frame 15). Which architectures use these: the cusp pair the deep0\_cusp columns, $\alpha$ every meta-GGA architecture, both the deep0\_cusp\_mgga one (the backup architecture table); none of them has an evaluated cell yet. Whether $\alpha$ is self-consistent: yes, it is recomputed from the live density matrix at every SCF cycle, not frozen from a reference. \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p6_1_tau_from_the_live_density_matrix} to \pageref{code:p6_5_the_cusp_pair_x_4_x_5}.}

\begin{frame}{1. Model: the network}
  \small
  Each of the exchange and the correlation networks is a multilayer perceptron of \texttt{depth} hidden layers of \texttt{nodes} units with GELU activations, read at every grid point; its scalar output $g$ becomes the enhancement factor on the next slide [code: networks.py:169, 379--404].
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GELU:} $\mathrm{GELU}(z) = z\,\Phi(z)$ with $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function, a smooth rectifier; the enhancement factors and their derivatives (the potential) are smooth in the density variables.
    \item \textbf{Last layer:} Glorot-initialized in the deep networks, zeroed in the deep0 networks, so the latter start exactly at $F = 1$ (the LDA) before pre-training.
    \item \textbf{Attention variants} (\texttt{\_attn}) [Vas17 Eq. 1; Xio20; code: net.py:55--136]: after the first hidden layer, the $H$ hidden units are $h$ tokens of dimension $d_k = H/h$ under pre-layer-norm self-attention across the token axis, a per-point re-mixing of hidden units; the functional stays semilocal.
  \end{itemize}
  \[
  \mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V, \qquad
  x \leftarrow x + W^{O}\,\mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{head}_1, \ldots, \mathrm{head}_h)(\mathrm{LN}(x)).
  \]
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the paragraph states the network: a multilayer perceptron of \texttt{depth} hidden layers of \texttt{nodes} units with GELU activations, evaluated independently at every quadrature point; its scalar output becomes the enhancement factor on the next slide. The bullets define GELU (the Gaussian error linear unit, $z\,\Phi(z)$, a smooth rectifier whose continuous derivative the potential inherits), the two starts (Glorot against zeroed last layer), and the attention variants: after the first hidden layer the $H$ hidden units are cut into $h$ tokens of dimension $d_k = H/h$, each token attends to the others with the scaled dot-product formula of the display line, and the result is added back after a layer normalization. \par \textbf{The point:} the networks are small (three layers of 16 or two of 8) and pointwise, so every functional here stays semilocal; attention re-mixes hidden units at one grid point and never looks at neighbouring points. \par \textbf{Terms:} multilayer perceptron, GELU, Glorot initialization, self-attention, tokens and heads, $d_k$, layer normalization, pre-layer-norm form, semilocal. \par \textbf{If asked:} how many heads: 4 for the 3x16 architectures, 2 for the 2x8 pair (frame 9). How many parameters: a 3x16 exchange network is an input layer of 16 units, two 16 x 16 layers and one output unit, 593 parameters with the biases (625 for correlation with its three inputs; networks.py:279 sets the input width); deep\_3x16 and deep0\_3x16 have the same count. Why GELU and not ReLU: the potential entering the SCF is the functional derivative of the energy and inherits the activation's smoothness; a ReLU kink would put a kink into the potential. \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p7_1_the_mlp_gelu_the_attention_block_after_t} to \pageref{code:p7_3_the_self_attention_block}.}

\begin{frame}{1. Model: the functional form}
  \small
  MLP output $g$ at a grid point:
  \[
  F_x = 1 + \mathcal{L}_{1.804}\!\left(G_x \cdot g_x\right), \qquad
  F_c = 1 + \mathcal{L}_{2}\!\left(G_c \cdot g_c\right),
  \]
  \[
  \mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(z) = \lambda\,\mathrm{sigmoid}\!\left(z - \ln(\lambda - 1)\right) - 1 \quad \text{[DFS21 Eq. 11]},
  \]
  \[
  G = \tanh^2(s) \ \ \text{(GGA)}, \qquad G = x_s + \tanh^2(x_\alpha) \ \ \text{(meta-GGA) [DFS21 Eq. 12]}.
  \]
  $\mathcal{L}_\lambda(0) = 0$ and $F \in (0, \lambda)$; $\lambda = 1.804 = 1 + \kappa_{\rm PBE}$ for exchange [PBE96 Eq. 14] (DFS uses 1.174 [PRSB14]), $\lambda = 2$ so that $F_c \ge 0$ [DFS21 Eq. 13]. The gate $G$ pins $F = 1$ where the gradient vanishes.
  \[
  E_{xc} = \tfrac12\left(E_x[2 n_\alpha] + E_x[2 n_\beta]\right) + E_c[n_\alpha, n_\beta] \ \ \text{[OP79; code: descriptors.py:124]},
  \]
  \[
  E_x[n] = \int n\, \epsilon_x^{\rm LDA}(n)\, F_x\, d^3r, \qquad
  E_c = \int n\, \epsilon_c^{\rm PW92}(r_s, \zeta)\, F_c\, d^3r \ \ \text{[PW92]}.
  \]
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the first display line writes the enhancement factors as one plus a bounded map of the gated network output; the second defines the map $\mathcal{L}_\lambda$, a shifted sigmoid that sends 0 to 0 and confines $F$ to $(0, \lambda)$; the third the gate $G$, $\tanh^2(s)$ on the GGA rung, with an $x_\alpha$ term on the meta-GGA rung. The text gives the ceilings: 1.804 for exchange, the PBE value derived from the Lieb-Oxford bound (DFS used 1.174), and 2 for correlation, which only guarantees $F_c \ge 0$. The last two lines assemble the energy: exchange from the exact spin-scaling relation of Oliver and Perdew (the spin-unpolarized functional at twice each spin density, averaged), correlation from PW92 times $F_c$. \par \textbf{The point:} the uniform electron gas limit $F = 1$ is exact by construction (the gate vanishes with the gradient) and the bounds of the map keep every trained functional inside the same envelope as PBE. \par \textbf{Terms:} enhancement factor, the bounded map, the gate, the Lieb-Oxford bound, the spin-scaling relation, PW92. \par \textbf{If asked:} why 1.804 and not DFS's 1.174: 1.804 is $1 + \kappa_{\rm PBE}$, PBE's own ceiling, so the clone of PBE can reach PBE's factor at every $s$; 1.174 is the tighter bound of Perdew, Ruzsinszky, Sun and Burke that DFS adopted. Whether the correlation ceiling matters: it binds for the attention networks: deep\_attn\_3x16 at 12 points saturates it ($F_c$ 1.99997 at $r_s = 5$ and $s = 5.4$; 1.95 at $r_s = 2$ and $s = 6$, trained\_fx\_fc\_curves.csv), where the training data has no say. Where the LDA and PW92 factors are computed: models.py:175--215 (the appendix). \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p8_1_the_bounded_map_l_lambda} to \pageref{code:p8_3_the_spin_scaled_exchange_energy_oliver_a}.}

\begin{frame}{2. Subsets: the metric [Lin91]}
  \small
  The distance between a candidate subset $S$ and the pool is the Jensen-Shannon divergence [code: subset\_selection.py:213--246, 472] summed over the three marginals with equal weights, $P_x$ the pool and $Q_x$ the subset mass function, probabilities floored at $10^{-12}$, natural logarithm:
  \[
  \mathrm{JSD}(S) = \sum_{x \in \{\rho^{1/3},\, s,\, \alpha\}} \tfrac12 \left[ \mathrm{KL}(P_x \,\|\, M_x) + \mathrm{KL}(Q_x \,\|\, M_x) \right], \qquad
  M_x = \tfrac12 (P_x + Q_x),
  \]
  \[
  \mathrm{KL}(P \,\|\, Q) = \sum_b P^{(b)} \ln \frac{P^{(b)}}{Q^{(b)}}, \qquad \text{each marginal term in } [0, \ln 2].
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The linear edges reach the low-density tails ($s$ to $2.0\times10^{10}$, $\alpha$ to $2.6\times10^{10}$, $\rho^{1/3}$ from $10^{-10}$ to 9.04), so the first bin holds 98.1, 88.9 and 97.2 percent of the weighted reference mass: the whole chemical range for $s$ and $\alpha$, the vacuum tail ($\rho < 9.2\times10^{-5}$) for $\rho^{1/3}$.
    \item The metric therefore compares the low-density tails of the molecular grids ($s$, $\alpha$) and the valence and core region ($\rho^{1/3}$); the $s$ term is 74 to 89 percent of every divergence.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the display lines define the metric: the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the pool's mass function $P_x$ and the candidate subset's $Q_x$, summed over the three marginals with equal weight, each term the symmetrized, bounded form of the Kullback-Leibler divergence (compared with the average $M$ of the two, so it lies between 0 and $\ln 2$ per marginal; the total between 0 and $3 \ln 2 = 2.08$), probabilities floored at $10^{-12}$ so that empty bins give no $\ln 0$, natural logarithm (the report's "nats"). The bullets state a property of the selection as it was run: the linear bin edges reach the low-density tails ($s$ to $2 \times 10^{10}$, $\alpha$ to $2.6 \times 10^{10}$), so the first bin holds 98.1, 88.9 and 97.2 percent of the weighted reference mass, the whole chemical range of $s$ and $\alpha$ and the vacuum tail of $\rho^{1/3}$, and the $s$ term is 74 to 89 percent of every divergence. \par \textbf{The point:} the metric compares subsets mainly on how their grids' low-density tails are distributed; this is stated rather than changed because every trained cell used these subsets. \par \textbf{Terms:} Kullback-Leibler divergence, Jensen-Shannon divergence, nat, the probability floor. \par \textbf{If asked:} why the tails are so heavy: on a molecular quadrature grid the density falls exponentially while its gradient and kinetic terms do not fall as fast, so $s$ and $\alpha$ reach $10^{10}$ in the outermost shells and stretch the linear range. Would log-spaced bins change the subsets: they would change the metric; the selection is not rerun because every trained cell rests on these subsets, and the property is stated in the report's Sec. 2.2. Was an alternative metric stored: the L2 distance was computed and stored beside the JSD and no run used it (frame 13). \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p12_1_the_probability_floor_1e_12} to \pageref{code:p12_3_the_jensen_shannon_divergence_over_the_t}.}

\begin{frame}{2. Subsets: the search}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item For each $r \in \{1,\ldots,7, 12, 15, 18, 26\}$ the subset is the exhaustive minimizer of $\mathrm{JSD}(S)$ over all $\binom{26}{r}$ combinations: 26 candidates at $r = 1$, 9.7 million at $r = 12$, one at $r = 26$ (the whole pool).
    \item Histograms add over disjoint samples, so every point's histogram is binned once and a candidate's mass function is a sum; a candidate with no grid point inside a marginal's range gets an infinite divergence and is never chosen.
    \item The subsets are nested only where the minimizer happens to nest: the 2-to-6-point subsets are barriers and ionizations with one or two molecules; from seven points on, atomization energies dominate (next slide).
    \item The L2 distance was computed and stored beside the JSD but no training run used it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} four bullets on the search: for every size $r$ the exhaustive minimizer over all $\binom{26}{r}$ combinations (26 at $r = 1$, 9.7 million at $r = 12$, one at $r = 26$); the additivity of histograms over disjoint samples, so every point's histogram is binned once and a candidate's mass function is a sum; the infinite-divergence rule for a candidate whose grids reach none of a marginal's range; the non-nesting of the minimizers and the composition of the small subsets; the stored L2 alternative. \par \textbf{The point:} the subsets are global minimizers, not greedy choices, and the small ones are barriers and ionizations because a barrier reaction contributes four or five species' grids and covers more of the pool's descriptor distribution than a single molecule. \par \textbf{Terms:} exhaustive search, nesting. \par \textbf{If asked:} what the search records: the ledger subset\_index\_log.json holds, for each size, the chosen indices and the divergence, and the figure script recomputes every value from the cached descriptors (frame 14). \section{3. Pre-training}

\begin{frame}{3. Pre-training: targets and objective}
  \small
  Each network is fitted pointwise [code: pretrain.py:261--341, pretrain\_data\_gen.py] to reproduce its parent (PBE on the GGA rung, SCAN on the meta-GGA) on the parent's own densities of the DFS pre-training inventory plus every atom of the BH76 and W4-11 pools (38 systems on the GGA rung, 36 on the meta-GGA; the certificate re-evaluates 39 and 38), with a synthetic $(r_s, s, \alpha)$ mesh [DFS21 SI] at 30 percent of the weight:
  \[
  t_x = F_x^{\rm parent} - 1 = \frac{\epsilon_x^{\rm parent}}{\epsilon_x^{\rm LDA}} - 1, \qquad
  t_c = F_c^{\rm parent} - 1 = \frac{\epsilon_c^{\rm parent}}{\epsilon_c^{\rm PW92}} - 1,
  \]
  the exchange rows posed per spin channel at the doubled density $(2 n_\sigma, 4\sigma_{\sigma\sigma})$ [OP79]. The objective is the integration-weighted pointwise residual plus a per-system energy term:
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}_{\rm pre} = {} & \frac{\sum_i w_i\,(F_i^{\rm NN} - 1 - t_i)^2}{\sum_i w_i}
  + w_E\,\frac{1}{N_{\rm sys}}\sum_{s}\Big(\sum_{i\in s} w^{\rm grid}_i\, n_i\,\epsilon_i^{\rm LDA}\, F_i^{\rm NN} - E_{xc,s}^{\rm parent}\Big)^2, \\
  & w_i = |n_i\, \epsilon_i^{\rm LDA}|\, w_i^{\rm grid}, \qquad w_E = 0.1 .
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The energy term is needed because the pointwise residual alone does not bound a system's energy.
    \item Adam, 20000 steps, learning rate $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$ from the midpoint, clip 1.0, a 20 percent validation slice scored every 50 steps with patience 300 checks [cfg \texttt{pretrain:}].
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the text states what pre-training fits: each network pointwise to its parent (PBE on the GGA rung, SCAN on the meta-GGA) on the parent's own densities of the DFS pre-training inventory plus every atom of the BH76 and W4-11 pools (38 systems on the GGA rung, 36 on the meta-GGA; the certificate re-evaluates 39 and 38), with a synthetic $(r_s, s, \alpha)$ mesh at 30 percent of the weight. The first display line gives the targets, the parent's enhancement factor minus one, evaluated with libxc, the exchange rows posed per spin channel at the doubled density. The objective is the integration-weighted pointwise residual (weights $|n\,\epsilon^{\rm LDA}|$ times the quadrature weight, i.e. how much each point contributes to the energy) plus a per-system energy term at $w_E = 0.1$. The bullets: why the energy term exists, and the optimizer settings (Adam, 20000 steps, the learning rate from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$ over the second half, clip 1.0, a 20 percent validation slice every 50 steps, patience 300 checks). \par \textbf{The point:} pre-training is not training on reference data; it makes the network produce the parent functional, so that every trained cell starts from PBE. \par \textbf{Terms:} parent functional, clone, the synthetic mesh, the energy term, patience. \par \textbf{If asked:} why the energy term: an earlier round showed that the architecture with the lowest exchange residual carried the largest atomization-energy offset, since pointwise errors that do not cancel between a molecule and its atoms add up; the term at $w_E = 0.1$ bounds the energy the pointwise residual does not (the report's Sec. 3.1). Why 30 percent for the mesh: it regularizes the fit where molecules sample the input space sparsely; the value is the run configuration's \texttt{pretrain:} block. How long a fit takes: 3.8 h for deep\_3x16 and 28 h for deep\_attn\_3x16 (frame 18). \par Code: appendix pp. \pageref{code:p15_1_the_pre_training_systems_the_dfs_invento} to \pageref{code:p15_10_the_loop_validation_every_validate_every}.}

\section{4. Optimization, in-sample}

\begin{frame}{4. In-sample: the training objective [DFS21 Eqs. 15--17]}
  \small
  One optimizer step per training group per epoch (a reaction, or a molecule with its density and potential references); the group loss is the DFS weighting ($\lambda_{RE} = 1$, $\lambda_n = 20$), with $E^{\rm NN}$ the self-consistent energy of the $N = 3$ cycle SCF and $t$ the cycle:
  \[
  \mathcal{L} = 1\cdot\mathcal{L}_{AE} + 1\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\rm rxn} + 1\cdot\mathcal{L}_{IP} + 1\cdot\mathcal{L}_{v_{xc}} + 20\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\rho},
  \]
  \[
  \mathcal{L}_{\rm rxn} = \frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{t\in T} w_t^2\, r_t^2, \qquad
  r_t = \sum_i c_i\, E^{\rm NN}_{i,t} - E^{\rm ref}, \qquad
  w_t = \left(\frac{t}{N-1}\right)^2,
  \]
  \[
  \mathcal{L}_{AE} = 0.01\,\mathrm{mean}_Z\, \frac{(E_Z^{\rm NN} - E_Z^{\rm anchor})^2}{(E_Z^{\rm anchor})^2}, \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{IP} = \mathrm{mean}\,(E^{\rm NN}_{+} - E^{\rm NN}_0 - IP^{\rm ref})^2, \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{v_{xc}} = \frac{\|V_{xc}^{\rm NN} - V_{xc}^{\rm ref}\|_F^2}{n_{\rm AO}^2},
  \]
  \[
  \mathcal{L}_{\rho} = \mathrm{mean}_{\rm mol}\,\frac{\sum_g w_g\,(n^{\rm NN}_g - n^{\rm ref}_g)^2}{N_e^2}, \qquad N_e = \sum_g w_g\, n^{\rm ref}_g .
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{L}_{\rm rxn}$ is the DFS convergence-tail scoring ($w_t^2 = 0, 1/16, 1$ at $N = 3$) over the W4-11 atomizations, as reactions with the network's own atoms, and the BH76 barriers [W411, GMTKN55]; the free atoms are held near their exact energies [Chak93]; $V_{xc}$ is compared on the reference density; the density against CCSD on the same grid, per electron. Code: losses.py:76, 366--396; train.py:1829--1835; oneshot.py:632--653.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\slidenote{\textbf{On the slide:} the objective of the training step, the only stage that changes the functional away from PBE. One optimizer step per training group per epoch, a group being a reaction or a molecule with its density and potential references. The first display line is the DFS weighting of the five channels ($\lambda_{RE} = 1$, $\lambda_n = 20$). $\mathcal{L}_{\rm rxn}$: the reaction residual $r_t$ formed from the network's self-consistent species energies at SCF cycle $t$ with stoichiometric coefficients $c_i$, weighted by the convergence tail $w_t^2 = 0, 1/16, 1$ at three cycles, the DFS device that penalizes an SCF that does not settle; the W4-11 atomizations enter here as reactions "molecule to atoms" with the network's own atoms, beside the BH76 barriers. $\mathcal{L}_{AE}$: the free atoms of the subset held near their exact non-relativistic energies as a relative error at the DFS weight 0.01, so the absolute energy scale cannot drift. $\mathcal{L}_{IP}$: cation minus neutral against the reference. $\mathcal{L}_{v_{xc}}$: the potential matrix against a reference potential from the inverted reference density, on the reference density, in the Frobenius norm (the square root of the sum of the squared matrix elements) over $n_{\rm AO}^2$; not part of the DFS objective. $\mathcal{L}_\rho$: the final SCF density against CCSD on the same grid, per electron squared. \par \textbf{The point:} the reaction and density channels are the Letter's, with its weights 1 and 20; the potential channel is an addition whose behaviour under training is reported on the next slide. \par \textbf{Terms:} group, epoch, convergence tail, atom anchor, the Frobenius norm, the potential channel. \par \textbf{If asked:} why the atomization energies are formed with the network's own atoms: an atomization energy against fixed exact atomic energies measures the network's absolute-energy offset, not the trained quantity (frame 26's note). \begin{frame}{5. Held-out: the set and the metrics [Goe17; DFS21 Eqs. 20, 21]}

  D_{\rm RMSE} = \sqrt{\sum_g w_g (n_g - n_g^{\rm ref})^2 \Big/ \sum_g w_g}.

    \item A cell beats PBE when its $\mathcal{ED}$ is below PBE's on the same slice. Pooled PBE: 8.92 kcal/mol WTMAD-2, $\varepsilon_n$ 0.00923.
