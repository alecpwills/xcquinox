### A1 -- V_xc (and DM) loss normalized by the PADDED AO count

Defect. losses.py `_vxc_term` divided the Frobenius error by
`n_ao = vxc_ref_arr.shape[-1]` on both the RKS branch and the UKS branch
(`2 * n_ao * n_ao`), and `_dm_term` divided by
`math.prod(dm_ref_arr.shape)` -- all three read the reference matrix AFTER
shape padding, so padding a molecule from n_ao to n_ao_t silently rescaled
its loss by (n_ao/n_ao_t)^2 species-dependently. Corrected magnitude (the
commit-time 4.7x figure was a mid-range pairing; the reviewer computed the
span over the pool's own species at 6-311++G(3df,2pd)): n_ao runs 15 (H,
which carries both vxc_ref and dm_target) to 117, worst case (15/117)^2 =
60.8x on the default pool (84.6x once the barrier mode's 138-AO
transition state enters; the vxc-supervised-span sub-figure was
retracted as unverifiable from local caches) -- while padding.py's
module docstring advertises the pass as "results-neutral". The `_dm_term` site was found by
the pre-edit caller sweep (`grep -n "n_ao" losses.py`), one site beyond the
two the plan named.

Change. `padding._pad_mol_data` records the physical AO count before any
padding as `mol_data["n_ao_unpadded"]`, a traced 0-d array (a Python int
leaf would re-key the JIT cache per molecule and defeat padding's purpose);
`_vxc_term` and `_dm_term` divide by it when present, with the shape
fallback unchanged for unpadded batches.

Evidence (test_vxc_padding_neutrality.py, quadrature-stub V_xc so the
numerator is padding-neutral by construction and any difference isolates
the denominator):
- RED (pytest_a1_red.log): `padding rescaled loss_vxc:
  0.0027495747254749903 vs 0.01718484203421869 (ratio 0.160000 ...)` --
  exactly (2/5)^2 at n_ao 2 -> 5; UKS branch identical; `KeyError:
  'n_ao_unpadded'`; DM twin (pytest_a1_red_dm.log): `ratio 0.160000`.
- GREEN: `4 passed` (pytest_a1_green.log).
- Module suites, each in its own process: test_losses.py `103 passed,
  3 xfailed`; test_losses_step7.py `31 passed`; test_shape_padding.py run

practical deviation of the shipped anchored starts was real but tiny,
and the defect's severity lies elsewhere.** Mechanism: the trainer
scored candidates only at validation steps >= 50 (step % every == 0), so
under `model.parent_anchor: true`, where the network at initialization
IS the parent (fitted objective at step 0: 2.72e-32, the argmin on all
eight G1 xnet/cnet trajectories), the true optimum was structurally
unselectable and the stage shipped the best post-init step instead --
1e5 to 1e18 times worse in the fitted objective. Every training cell
loads that shipped checkpoint as its starting weights
(spec_builder.py:623), so the campaign's controlled-comparison premise
("the networks equal the parent at initialization") held only
approximately. The magnitude, measured at certificate scale: 7.2e-4 mHa
against the 1.0 kcal/mol-class tolerance -- the G1 starting functionals
were the parent to well below every physical scale in play, and no
observed training behavior of the prior record is attributable to this
defect. Its real costs were: the broken selection contract (a
correctness defect regardless of magnitude); wasted schedule (an
anchored fit that never improves now patiences out at step 500 of 2500
and ships the init, instead of running the full 2500 to ship a worse
network); and the masked-divergence hazard the refusal now guards -- a
diverging fit beside an exact init previously shipped the divergent
network's best step, and a REFUSED diverged run left its stale val-best
checkpoints on disk for the pull to carry (both now fixed and pinned).

**3. The V_xc channel trained with padded-shape denominators at full
channel weight.** The per-molecule scheme forces the vxc pre-weight to
1.0 (xcquinox/alec/train.py:1836-1837), so the V_xc channel carried the
same weight as each energy channel, and its per-species normalization
divided by the run-wide PADDED AO count: between-species mis-weighting
of (n_ao_pad / n_ao_own)^2, executed per cell as 21.2x (jsd/1) and 60.8x
(every other cell; H at 15 AOs vs N2O/CO2 at 117). The density channel
(weight 20.0, per-electron) was padding-neutral by construction
(zero-padded quadrature weights; bit-identical padded vs unpadded in all
three normalization modes, in-tree oracle
test_grid_term_is_padding_neutral). The padding design itself -- one
common JIT signature to hold XLA compile memory down -- is retained; the
fix divides by the stashed true AO count.

**4. The Dick atomic regularizer anchored atoms outside its allowlist.**
With `regularize_atom_syms=('H','Li')` configured, a group whose
single-atom species carried neither symbol fell back to
regularize-everything (the `scoped_reg or None` collapse): the O atom in
OH+CH3_to_O+CH4, the F atom in HF+F_to_H+F2, and C/C+ in C_IP were
pulled toward reference energies at w_atomic 0.01 inside the AE channel,
in every cell from jsd/3 upward. Fixed: an empty scope stays empty, with
a positive control pinning that a configured symbol still derives its
anchor.

### The correlation growth: what bounds it, what it violates, what it does

The unanchored clone's correlation output is `F_c = 1 + LOB(tanh^2(s) *
MLP)` with `LOB(x) = 2 sigma(x - ln 1) - 1` (networks.py:105, 634-642;
`resolved_cnet_lob_lim` 2.0 at config.py:335; `c_constraints=()` by default
at config.py:115; the medium class is depth 3 x 16 at config.py:508;
`parent_anchor: false` in the run's resolved config). So F_c lies in (0, 2)
with F_c(s = 0) = 1 and nothing else: no upper bound at 1, no decay as
s -> infinity, no r_s -> 0 condition. The published clone uses the same
map ("F_c = 1 + I_2[x_2 F_c]", "I_a(x) = a/(1 + (a - 1) exp(-x)) - 1") and
says of it: "The upper bound on the correlation enhancement factor is not
physically required; it is imposed purely as a regularization to prevent
unphysical extrapolations during training." (arXiv:2605.10331, Sect.
II.1); no rapidly-varying-limit condition is listed there either.

Against that, PBE's own factor (parents.py:267-273) has H >= 0, hence
F_c^PBE <= 1, and H -> -eps_c^unif as t -> infinity, hence F_c^PBE -> 0
(0.002 at s = 6, r_s = 2 on the drawn slice). An F_c of 1.2-1.4 at s = 6
therefore breaks no theorem -- E_c <= 0 holds (F_c > 0), the second-order
gradient expansion's sign is respected (the ss=5 curve dips below 1 at
small s, 0.994 at s = 0.1 against PBE's 0.989, with about half PBE's
coefficient, which no class enforces) -- but it breaks two of PBE's design
conditions (Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof, PRL 77, 3865 (1996)): H >= 0, and
correlation vanishing in the rapidly varying limit. Physically it deepens
correlation in exactly the regions where gradient corrections exist to
remove LDA's roughly twofold overestimate of atomic |E_c|.

What the trained functional does with it, from the per-molecule energies
of the validation-best checkpoints (E(TS) - sum E(reactants), and the W4-11
atomization shifts, kcal/mol, computed from per_reaction / per_molecule
of spec_0004 and spec_0000):

| cell | BH76 TS-minus-reactants shift (mean / median / trimmed) | W4-11 atomization shift (mean / median) |
|---|---|---|
| ss=5 | +7.46 / +5.66 / +4.76 (n = 59; RKT17 +296, hoch3fts -129) | -29.5 / -27.2 (n = 120) |
| ss=1 | +0.98 / +0.43 / +0.35 | -8.74 / -8.53 |

The transition states are RAISED relative to reactants, not lowered: the
barrier correction (BH76 signed -7.05 -> +0.42 at ss=5, at the cost of MAE
7.32 -> 11.76) and the Z-proportional positive drift of every total energy
cannot come from F_c > 1, which only lowers energies; they come from the
exchange side (the s = 0.8-1.0 dip and the raised saturation). The
correlation growth acts where the density is low and s is large: for a
tail rho ~ exp(-2 sqrt(2I) r) at I = 0.5 Ha, s = 2.5 is reached near rho =
2e-3 and s = 6 near rho = 1.6e-4 a.u. (r_s ~ 11, eps_c^PW92 ~ -0.017 Ha,
estimate), so a dF_c of +0.3 deposits of order -3 kcal/mol per electron of
tail charge. Free atoms and radicals carry more tail charge than compact
bonded molecules, so the term lowers atoms more than molecules and reduces
atomization energies -- the direction measured (PBE overbinds the training
atomizations by +11.4 mean; ss=1 moves them by -8.7 and lands under PBE,
ss=5 by -29.5 and overshoots). The evaluation JSON carries no
exchange/correlation split, so this attribution is by consistency; the
quantitative version is sum_i w_i rho_i eps_c^PW92 (F_c^NN - F_c^PBE)
binned in s per system class on the pretraining grid files, which live on
the cluster.

Interventions, ranked by physical defensibility, none applied:

1. The pointwise cloning penalty during training. The hook exists --
   `pbe_anchor_weight` / `pbe_anchor_sample` are injected into the loss
   (train.py:1482-1483; pbe_anchor.py) and sit at 0.0 in the v7 YAMLs and
   the resolved snapshot -- but penalizes |F_x^NN - F_x^PBE|^2 only; it
   would be extended to F_c. No class change, no re-clone. Validation:
   F_c(s) within tolerance of the certificate curve after training, and
   the W4-11 signed error.
2. A rapidly-varying-limit gate tying F_c's large-s decay to the parent's
   (networks.py:642). Class change, so every architecture re-clones
   (1.6-28 h per pretrain). A PBE design condition, not an exact one.
3. A one-sided map F_c <= 1 (networks.py:105/642, config.py:335). Class
   change and re-clone; same status as 2.
4. Anchoring the correlation channel only (networks.py:639-640;
   config.py:159/480, where `anchored()` sets both nets). Class change;
   the anchored v6 class collapsed the same correction past s = 2.
5. A smaller fine-tuning rate (`lr_start` 1e-3 in the resolved snapshot;
   train.py:163-181). Slows the lever without removing it.

The first is the one to run first: it keeps the certified clone as the
prior at zero pretraining cost and penalizes exactly the region where the
density and V_xc channels carry no weight; if it cannot hold F_c at s = 6
without degrading the in-sample fit, the gate (2) is the class-level fix.
