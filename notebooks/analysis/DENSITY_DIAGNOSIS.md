# Why the held-out densities do not improve on the cell-level species mean -- diagnosis

The training weights match the DFS Letter's (density channel at 20x the energy channels;
see `LOSS_PRIMER.md`), yet the network densities do not beat PBE ON THE CELL-LEVEL
SPECIES MEAN -- the reduction every count in this note uses; the per-species
counter-statistic is recorded under "Not universal failure" below, and on the anchored
v6 record the per-species direction reverses outright (spec_0021 val-best: median NN/PBE
Eq.-20 ratio 0.898, NN better on 150 of 199 species). This note records what
the pulled production run shows. Every number is regenerated by
`notebooks/analysis/density_diagnosis_evidence.py` (reads pulled artifacts only -- no
SCF, no model load); the run analyzed is `run_20260728T140018Z` (dfs6311_grid3_v3, 18
completed specs, 42950 optimizer updates), with cross-basis checks against the pulled
def2-svp runs.

## Short answer

The density channel exerts no effective optimization pressure. Its value is flat across
200 epochs (median first-to-last ratio 0.998, and flat under every window and regression
tested -- Sec. 1), and where the open-shell radicals CH and NO are in the training set
they own 68-98% of the channel with an error the functional cannot close: model-free PBE
reproduces it to within 0.4%.

That error is a **degenerate-component mismatch**, not functional error and not strong
correlation. The evidence is decisive (Sec. 3): across every pulled run, the model-free
PBE density error is bit-identical for all 20 closed-shell species and varies -- up to
23x -- for exactly three, CH, HO and NO, the three open-shell 2-Pi radicals. They vary at
constant energy (CH's PBE total energy spans 8.2e-08 Ha while its density error spans
20x). Same energy, different density is a degenerate manifold: the SCF settles on a
different member of it than the stored reference did.

The consequence is that these runs are effectively energy-only training with a
decorative density term. The Letter's central claim is that density supervision improves
densities; ours exerts no pressure, so this run does not test that claim.

What held-out density we do observe tracks ARCHITECTURE, not density supervision:
deep_attn_3x16 reaches 0.968-1.052 and deep_3x16 1.030-1.141 (permutation test on the
7-vs-7 medians, p = 0.0118). The weights, checkpoint selection, held-out contamination,
and the 3-cycle SCF were each measured and cleared (Sec. 5).

## 1. The density channel does not decrease during training

Comparing the first five to the last five epochs of every spec's `aux_log.pkl`, the
density channel's median first-to-last ratio over 18 specs is **0.998**, and it rises in
9 of 18. Over the same 200 epochs `loss_AE` falls by factors of 15.9x to 198398x
(ratio column 0.000-0.063) and `loss_BH76` to 0.271-0.784x. The density channel ends up
occupying 15-94% of the loss budget not because it grew in importance but because every
other channel collapsed around it while it stood still.

The finding is not an artifact of the comparison window. Widening it, or regressing over
the whole trajectory, makes the channel look slightly WORSE, never better:

    window  5 epochs: median ratio 0.9982   rose in  9/18
    window 10 epochs: median ratio 0.9995   rose in  9/18
    window 20 epochs: median ratio 1.0044   rose in 10/18
    window 50 epochs: median ratio 1.0036   rose in 10/18
    OLS over all epochs: median relative slope 2.394e-05/epoch
                         => median total change +0.48% across 200 epochs
    Spearman(epoch, loss_rho): median +0.472, negative in only 8/18

A channel that is flat while it dominates the loss is the signature of a term whose
gradient cannot move it -- not of a term the optimizer is trading away.

## 2. Two open-shell radicals own the channel

Per-group attribution over the last five epochs (each training group takes one optimizer
step per epoch):

    spec_0006:  ae:CH  7.5231e-04   97.7% of the channel   (99.9% of its own step's loss)
                ae:HO  4.2713e-06    0.6%
    spec_0008:  ae:CH  7.575e-04     68.8% of the channel
                ae:NO  2.921e-04     26.5%
    spec_0010:  ae:CH  7.553e-04     67.6%
                ae:NO  2.922e-04     26.2%

CH alone accounts for 67.6-97.7% of the channel depending on the cell, and where NO is
also present the two together account for ~94%. Both are frozen (CH x0.995 in spec_0006,
x1.012 in spec_0016 over 200 epochs). The `ae:CH` optimizer step is 99.9% density term:
one step per epoch spends itself almost entirely on this single residual.

This is also why the channel jumps 30x between subset sizes 5 and 6 (CH enters there) and
why the earlier framing "one species is the channel" is wrong for the three largest cells
-- NO is a second offender, and any remedy that misses it leaves a quarter of the problem
in place.

## 3. The mechanism: degenerate-component mismatch, not physics

In-sample per-molecule density for spec_0006 (`eval/per_molecule.json`), Eq. 20
per-electron L1 against the CCSD references:

    molecule     eps_NN      eps_PBE    NN/PBE
    CH         1.5551e-01  1.5493e-01   1.004    <-- 18x the population median
    NO (spec_0008 pool)    7.7782e-02   ~1.0     <--  9x
    HO         8.9289e-03  8.0967e-03   1.103
    N2         6.5690e-03  6.0078e-03   1.093
    CO2        9.4457e-03  9.4663e-03   0.998
    (population median 8.41e-03)

Model-free PBE reproduces CH's error to 0.4%, so the functional is not going to close it
-- that is the operative fact for training regardless of cause. But the cause is
determinable from the pulled data, and it is not what one would first guess.

`density_rmse_pbe` is model-free: a fixed functional against a fixed reference file. It
therefore CANNOT vary from spec to spec unless the SCF itself lands somewhere different.
Pooling every pulled def2-svp evaluation:

    species    n    rho min      rho max    max/min   PBE total-energy spread (Ha)
    HO       208  2.4465e-04   5.6179e-03   22.963            1.106e-06
    CH       114  1.6492e-04   3.3076e-03   20.057            8.166e-08
    NO        50  7.3639e-04   5.8861e-03    7.993            4.408e-08
    N2       208  3.1730e-04   3.1730e-04    1.000            5.116e-13
    CH4      161  2.4921e-04   2.4921e-04    1.000            2.842e-13
    ... 18 further closed-shell species, all 1.000 ...

Exactly three species scatter, and they are exactly the three open-shell 2-Pi radicals in
the pool. Every closed-shell species is bit-identical across all specs and runs. The
scatter happens at essentially constant energy -- CH's PBE total energy varies by
8.2e-08 Ha (5e-05 kcal/mol) while its density error varies 20-fold. Same energy,
different density is the definition of a degenerate manifold.

Two further observations close it:

- Pooled over 114 CH evaluations the density error ranges 1.6492e-04 to 3.3076e-03, and
  **4 draws land at or below the closed-shell population median** (the smallest is 0.61x
  that median). A GGA sometimes reproduces CH's stored CCSD density BETTER than it
  reproduces a typical closed-shell molecule's. That is impossible if the gap were
  genuine strong-correlation error -- a fixed functional against a fixed reference would
  give one number -- and is exactly what component mismatch produces.
- The dfs6311 run's own YAML says this is what the orientation lock is for
  (`hpcjobs/configs/dfs_step7.dfs6311_grid3_v3.yaml:132-133`): "Orientation lock
  (run-level, authoritative): locks OH/CH/NO reference + training densities to the SAME
  degenerate component". Under the lock all three become bit-stable in the 6311 run --
  but only HO landed on the component its reference used (1.3564e-04, ordinary), while
  CH (1.5758e-03) and NO (2.5666e-03) did not. **The lock achieved reproducibility, not
  agreement.**

So the mechanism is a methodology defect, and it is specific: the lock pins the SCF to a
reproducible degenerate component without guaranteeing it is the component the stored
reference was generated on. Dick & Fernandez-Serra met the same wall and handled it by
scaling `lambda_n` by 0.01 for CH and OH.

Scope of this conclusion: it is established at def2-svp, where repeated independent
evaluations make the test possible. The 6-311++G(3df,2pd) references were not pulled, so
the direct confirmation that CH's and NO's 6311 references sit on a different component
than the 6311 SCF is OPEN -- Sec. 6 gives the commands. What the 6311 data does show is
consistent with it: the lock removed the scatter but left CH and NO at 10-18x the
population while HO became ordinary.

## 4. What the held-out outcome does and does not track

CH is in every cell with subset_size >= 6 and in none below (per each spec's
`train_metadata.json`); NO joins at ss = 15. It is tempting to conclude that the poisoned
cells are the ones with bad densities. **The data refutes that**, and recording the
refutation bounds what the artifact explains. Held-out density (median NN/PBE eps, final
checkpoint) against CH presence, split by architecture:

    deep_3x16        clean (1 cell) : 1.076
    deep_3x16        CH    (6 cells): 1.030 1.053 1.087 1.121 1.139 1.141   median 1.104
    deep_attn_3x16   clean (5 cells): 0.996 1.027 1.040 1.049 1.052        median 1.040
    deep_attn_3x16   CH    (2 cells): 0.968 0.998                          median 0.983

The two cells with the best densities in the run are CH-containing cells. The outcome
separates by ARCHITECTURE -- deep_attn median 1.027 vs deep_3x16 1.087, permutation test
on the 7-vs-7 medians p = 0.0118 -- and that conclusion does not rest on the n=1 and n=2
sub-cells above, which are too small to support any within-arch claim.

Within-architecture trends against subset size are non-monotone and should not be quoted
as trends: deep_3x16 runs 1.076, 1.030, 1.053, 1.121, 1.141, 1.139, 1.087 across
ss = 5..26 (r = +0.53, n = 7); deep_attn_3x16 is U-shaped, 0.996, 1.027, 1.049, 1.052,
1.040, 0.999, 0.968 (Spearman -0.21).

So the honest scope: the density channel is not doing its job anywhere -- flat
everywhere, and 68-98% irreducible where CH and NO are present -- which means the density
behaviour we observe is whatever energy training happens to produce. What this does NOT
establish is that repairing the channel would improve held-out densities. That is the
open experimental question (Sec. 7), not a settled consequence.

## 5. What was checked and cleared

- **The training weights.** Recovered from the logs by least squares with nothing
  assumed: (1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 20.0000) for
  (AE, BH76, IP13, vxc, rho), residual 4.0e-16, over all 42950 updates of all 18 specs,
  every entry stamped `update_scheme: per_molecule`. The test has power -- perturbing the
  density weight to 19 or 21 raises the residual to 5.0e-02. The Letter's 1/20 structure
  is what runs.
- **Held-out contamination.** No held-out species is at artifact scale: over 199 species
  the PBE eps median is 9.09e-03 and the maximum 2.15e-02, versus CH's 1.55e-01. The
  held-out density measurement is clean. (CH and NO are training species; NO also appears
  in the held-out pool but at ordinary error there.)
- **Checkpoint selection.** Median-of-medians NN/PBE eps over the 14 specs with all three
  evals: final 1.0503, train-best 1.0520, val-best 1.0488 -- a -0.14% difference. A
  paired per-spec test agrees on magnitude (median difference +0.00000) but not on sign
  (val-best better in 4/14 specs; per-species 926/1998 rows favour val-best, p = 0.0012,
  i.e. the FINAL checkpoint wins slightly more rows). Read as: selection is not a driver
  here, not as val-best being better. This supersedes the 1.066 -> 1.112 figure quoted on
  2026-07-29, which was the RMSE channel on `run_20260723T161502Z` (reproduced: 1.0661 ->
  1.1119); on the same current run the RMSE channel gives 1.1507 -> 1.1173, so the metric
  choice matters more than the checkpoint.
- **The 3-cycle SCF.** 91.3% of held-out rows are nominally unconverged, and the median
  last-step energy residual is 1.285e-05 Ha (0.008 kcal/mol). That bound clears the
  ENERGY channel only: a small energy residual does not bound the density-space distance
  to the converged fixed point, and the PBE comparator is model-free on a converged
  density, so the capped SCF is NOT cleared for the density leg -- a 25-cycle re-scoring
  of both legs is the discriminating test
  (`notebooks/analysis/rescore_depth_symmetric.py`).
- **An energy-density trade-off.** NOT DETECTED, with the caveat that the test has little
  power: Pearson r = +0.18 between per-cell energy and density ratios is t = 0.62 on 12
  df (p ~ 0.55), so a moderate negative correlation cannot be excluded from 14 cells.
  What can be said is that the best energy cell (spec_0017, 0.595) is also the best
  density cell (0.968).
- **Not universal failure.** 3 of 14 specs beat PBE on held-out density at the final
  checkpoint (spec_0011 0.996, spec_0016 0.999, spec_0017 0.968). Pooled over species
  rows: final median 1.0618 with 24.30% of rows better than PBE; val-best 1.0590 and
  24.41%.

## 6. Open: direct confirmation at the production basis

The 6-311++G(3df,2pd) references were not pulled, so the component-agreement check runs
on the cluster. Read-only, login node, seconds -- following the probe pattern of
`hpcjobs/dfs6311_c2_ref_probe.py`:

```bash
# CH and NO are the offenders, HO is the control that landed correctly.
# allow_pickle is required only because these npz files store string stamps as
# object arrays; they are our own generated caches on our own scratch.
python - <<'EOF'
import numpy as np, os, time
for base in ("/gpfs/scratch/awills/external_refs_dfs_6311ppg3df2pd_g3",
             "/gpfs/scratch/awills/external_refs_bench_6311ppg3df2pd_g3"):
    for s in ("CH", "NO", "HO", "N2"):
        p = os.path.join(base, f"{s}.npz")
        if not os.path.isfile(p):
            print(f"{base}/{s}: absent"); continue
        z = np.load(p, allow_pickle=True)
        get = lambda k: (z[k].item() if k in z.files and z[k].shape == () else
                         (z[k] if k in z.files else None))
        print(f"{os.path.basename(base)}/{s}: "
              f"lock={get('orientation_lock_strength')} basis={get('basis_used')} "
              f"grid={get('grid_level_used')} oep_conv={get('oep_converged')} "
              f"oep_err={get('oep_density_error')} n_e={get('oep_n_electrons')} "
              f"mtime={time.ctime(os.stat(p).st_mtime)}")
EOF
```

**The decisive field is whether the `orientation_lock_strength` key is PRESENT, not what
its value is.** The wiring is correct by construction and is already guarded: the
run-level `inputs.orientation_lock_strength` is authoritative and is threaded into the
SolverConfig the training SCF reads (`cluster/spec_builder.py:283-287`, "Run-level
inputs.orientation_lock_strength is authoritative (so the SCF matches the references)";
consumed at `train.py:354` and `:498`), both sides call the same operator
(`data.py` and `external_refs.py` both import `orientation_lock_bias`), and
`data.py:124-134` RAISES when a reference's stamped strength disagrees with the
consumer's. That guard did not fire on the production run, so "the two sides used
different lock strengths" is already excluded.

But the guard fires only when the reference carries the key -- its own comment says so:
"Fires only when BOTH the ref carries the key and the caller passes a lock". A reference
written before the stamp existed has no key and passes silently. Every reference in the
local def2-svp cache is in exactly that state (CH, HO, NO, N2: key ABSENT), consistent
with their pre-lock June generation.

So the check has three outcomes:

- **Key ABSENT on CH/NO** -- a locked SCF was trained against an unlocked reference, with
  the guard structurally unable to see it. That is the defect: regenerate those
  references, and extend the guard to treat a MISSING key as an error whenever the
  consumer resolves a nonzero lock, so it cannot recur.
- **Key PRESENT and equal to 3e-05 on CH/NO as on HO** -- the lock was applied
  consistently on both sides and the mismatch has another cause: either the 3e-05 bias
  does not split CH's and NO's manifolds the way it splits OH's (species-dependent
  efficacy; the window was sized on OH/NO), or CH's CCSD reference is not a
  single-component density any single-determinant SCF can match. Down-weighting is then
  the right response rather than regeneration.
- **Key PRESENT and different** -- impossible; the run would have aborted at the guard.

## 7. Remediation, ranked

None of these may be applied to the running array; each is a new sweep YAML and spec.

1. **Per-species density weighting** -- the Letter's own remedy, `lambda_n` x 0.01, but
   applied to **CH and NO**, not the Letter's CH and OH. In this run OH is the member of
   the trio that landed on the correct component and behaves normally (8.10e-03); CH and
   NO are the two that did not. This is the cheapest change, needs no regeneration, and
   is correct whether or not the lock is subsequently fixed.
2. **A channel-homogeneity guard.** The condition that hid this for a full sweep is
   detectable in one line: a species exceeding the channel population by an order of
   magnitude. Emitting it at spec-build or first-epoch time, naming the species, would
   have surfaced this on day one rather than after 18 completed cells.
3. **Fix the lock's component agreement** (the root cause, pending Sec. 6): make the
   reference generation and the training/eval SCF provably select the same degenerate
   component, verified per species instead of assumed. Note the lock already delivers
   reproducibility -- the 6311 run has zero cross-spec scatter -- so this is a narrower
   fix than it sounds.

The A/B that would answer the question Sec. 4 leaves open -- does a working density
channel improve held-out densities -- should take a subset_size >= 6 cell in BOTH
architectures, apply remedy (1), and compare against the corresponding cells of this run
as control. Both architectures are needed because the density outcome currently tracks
architecture, and a single-arch test could not separate the two effects.
